SubSleuth — Privacy Policy

Effective 24 November 2026.

SubSleuth is a subscription finder. It looks through a Gmail inbox you own and shows you the subscriptions you're paying for, when they renew, and where to cancel them. This policy explains what it touches and what it doesn't.

What SubSleuth accesses

Only when you click Connect Gmail and sign in with Google, SubSleuth reads your Gmail — read-only — to find subscription receipts, renewals and trials, and to read the unsubscribe link from newsletter emails. It uses Google's Gmail API and Google Identity Services to do this. It never sends, deletes, or changes any email.

Where your data goes

Nowhere. Everything is read and parsed in your own browser, on your own device. No email content is uploaded to, or stored on, any server. SubSleuth has no backend and no database. Close the tab and it's gone.

What SubSleuth does not do

  • It does not create an account for you and does not store your email address.
  • It does not track you, run analytics, or use advertising cookies.
  • It does not share your inbox or its contents with anyone.
  • It does not read your whole inbox — it searches for billing and subscription mail only.

Sample mode

Sample mode needs no sign-in and touches no real data. It runs entirely on built-in demo emails.

Google

Signing in and reading Gmail go through Google's own services. Google's privacy policy applies to that sign-in and to Google's handling of your account. SubSleuth only asks Google for read-only access to Gmail, and only when you choose to connect.

Revoking access

You can disconnect Gmail inside SubSleuth at any time. You can also revoke its access in your Google Account under Security → Third-party access. Once revoked, SubSleuth can no longer read your inbox.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date above will be updated.
